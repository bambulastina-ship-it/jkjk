01 Thesis

Dubai is not one market. It is a few dozen small ones that happen to share a skyline, and they do not move together.

A tower two streets from another can have a different service charge, a different tenant profile, a different handover record and a completely different resale depth. Averages published for “Dubai” flatten all of that. I do not underwrite on averages. I underwrite building by building, and I say no far more often than I say yes.

Most of what reaches a buyer publicly has already been priced by the time they see it. The interesting entries are earlier and quieter: an owner who needs a clean exit, a completion date that no longer suits someone, an assignment that has to move this month. Those do not go on a portal. They travel by phone, and only to people who answer quickly and do not waste the seller's time.

I hold Dubai property myself. That is the honest reason I read it the way I do — I am looking at the same downside my clients are, and I would rather show you a deal I would take than a deal that is simply available.

02 What I source

Three ways in, and none of them are the portal.

A

Off-market stock

Units that are genuinely available but never advertised — owners who do not want a listing, a viewing schedule, or their neighbours knowing the price. I hear about them through the people who actually hold the keys: other brokers I trade with, building managers, previous sellers. The advantage is not a discount by default. It is a quieter negotiation with fewer parties bidding against you.

B

Distressed and time-bound exits

Sellers with a deadline: a payment plan that has become inconvenient, a relocation, an assignment that must transfer before a milestone. These are real and they are fast. Speed and clean funds are the whole edge, so I will tell you plainly whether you are positioned to move at that pace before we start looking. If you are not, this is not the right category for you.

C

Resale, assignment and income stock

Completed or near-complete units bought to be held and let, or to be re-sold into a stronger window. Here the work is unglamorous: service charges, chiller arrangements, actual achieved rents in that specific building, how long comparable units sat before they moved, and what the exit looks like if the plan changes. I would rather kill a deal at this stage than after transfer.

What I do not do: promise a return, quote a yield I cannot evidence for a specific unit, or sell you something because it happens to be on my list this week.

03 Who I work with

Honest about fit, in both directions.

First Dubai purchase, from abroad
You have bought property before, elsewhere, and you want someone in the market who will explain the mechanics — ownership zones, transfer, payment structures, what the ongoing costs actually are — without being sold to on the same call.
Adding a Dubai line to an existing portfolio
You already hold real estate and you are comparing this market against others on its own terms. You want building-level reasoning and a clear statement of what you are exposed to, not a brochure.
Repeat buyers who want first look
You move quickly, you know your criteria, and what you want from me is to be on the short call list when something time-bound comes up — with enough context to decide the same day.
Probably not a fit
If you are looking for a family home to live in, you will be better served by an agent who specialises in that. And if what you want is a guaranteed return, I cannot give you one — nobody honestly can, and anyone who does should worry you.

The finish is the easy part. The question is what the building does for you in year five.

04 Engagement

How it runs.

  1. Step 01

    A call, before anything else

    What you are trying to achieve, over what horizon, and what you are actually comfortable holding. If Dubai is the wrong answer for that, I will say so on this call.

  2. Step 02

    A written brief

    Budget band, hold period, income versus capital, tolerance for construction risk, and the buildings and areas we are ruling out. Everything after this is measured against that page.

  3. Step 03

    Shortlist and viewings

    A small number of options with the reasoning attached, including what is wrong with each. Viewings in person, or on video with me walking the unit if you are not in the country.

  4. Step 04

    Offer, transfer, handover

    Negotiation, paperwork through the developer or the Land Department as applicable, and coordination with your conveyancer or mortgage broker through to keys. I stay on it after transfer — letting and management questions do not stop at completion.
