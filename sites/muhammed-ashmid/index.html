01

What “off-plan” actually means

Most of the confusion around Dubai property is vocabulary, not money. Here is the plain version, before anyone talks to you about a specific building.

Off-plan

A property bought before it is finished — sometimes before the ground is broken. You are buying from the developer against approved drawings, floor plans and renders, with a stated handover period. You choose your unit from a layout rather than by walking through it.

Ready

A completed, handed-over property. It exists, it can be inspected, surveyed and valued as it stands, and it can be lived in or let straight away. What you see on the viewing is what you own.

Handover

The point where a finished off-plan unit is transferred to you and you get the keys. Developers publish a target handover period; construction timelines can move, and any honest advisor will tell you that up front.

Payment plan

Off-plan purchases are normally structured in instalments tied to construction milestones rather than paid in one go. The structure is set by the developer and differs project to project — it is one of the first things to read carefully.

Escrow

In Dubai, buyer money for an off-plan project goes into a regulated project escrow account rather than straight to the developer. It is a core reason the off-plan market here is structured the way it is.

Snagging

The inspection at handover where defects are listed and sent back to the developer to fix. Worth budgeting time for; worth not skipping.

No figures appear anywhere on this page. Returns, prices and payment structures are specific to a project and a moment, and they belong in a conversation about your situation — not in a headline.

02

How a purchase runs

Six stages, in order. Nothing is skipped and nothing is rushed past you.

  1. 1

    The brief

    What the property is for — to live in, to let, to hold. Which areas you are open to. What the timeline looks like on your side. Everything after this depends on it.

  2. 2

    A shortlist, not a catalogue

    A small set of projects or ready units that genuinely answer the brief, with the reasoning for each one written out — including what is wrong with them.

  3. 3

    Reading the project

    Developer, location, what is actually being delivered, the handover period, the payment structure, the service charges, the exit routes. This is the stage that separates a good purchase from a regretted one.

  4. 4

    Reservation & paperwork

    Unit selection, the reservation form, the sale and purchase agreement, and the registration steps. You get the documents explained in plain language before you sign anything — never after.

  5. 5

    Through construction

    For off-plan, the long middle. Milestone updates, instalment dates, and a straight answer whenever a timeline moves. You should not have to chase for news.

  6. 6

    Handover & after

    Snagging, keys, and the decision about what happens next — move in, furnish, or let. The relationship does not end at the transfer.

03

Off-plan or ready — honestly

Neither one wins. They answer different questions, and the right answer is the one that fits your timeline and your appetite for waiting.

Off-plan Ready
When you get the keys
At handover, after construction. A wait, with a target date that can move.
On transfer. Weeks, not years.
What you can inspect
Drawings, floor plans, specifications and renders. Sometimes a show unit.
The actual property, the actual building, the actual view from the actual window.
How you pay
Instalments tied to construction milestones, set by the developer.
Settled at purchase, or through a mortgage arranged in the usual way.
Choice of unit
Widest at launch — floor, aspect, layout are still open.
Whatever is genuinely available in that building today.
Income timing
Nothing until handover; the property has to exist first.
Can be let once it is yours, subject to the usual tenancy rules.
The real trade-off
Patience and trust in a delivery, in exchange for choice and a staged structure.
Certainty and speed, from a smaller pool of what already exists.

If someone answers “which is better?” without asking about your timeline first, they are selling, not advising.

04

How to read a render

Off-plan is sold with pictures of something that does not exist yet. Knowing what a render is — and what it is not — is the single most useful habit a buyer can build.

  • a

    Renders are intent, not evidence. Planting is mature, the sky is perfect, the cars are staged. Read the geometry and the layout; discount the styling.

  • b

    Finishes are specified in writing, not in pixels. The material schedule in the contract is what you are owed. Anything only visible in an image is decoration.

  • c

    Check what is outside the frame. Renders crop. What sits on the plot next door matters as much as the façade you are being shown.

  • d

    Ask what stage it is at. Concept, approved design and as-built are three different things wearing the same picture.

Who this is for

First time in Dubai

Buying to hold
